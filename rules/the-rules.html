Dictating Rolls

Generally, a Skill is obvious given the task. However, an Attribute is usually a given method of accomplishing the task, and it can be argued for by the player or GM, with the GM being the final decider.

Die Size Steps

The ladder is d4 -> d6 -> d8 -> d10 -> d12. An effect granting +1 Die Size moves one step up the ladder, while -1 Die Size moves it downward. d4 and d12 are the respective floor and ceiling.

Rolls

Epitaph uses two types of rolls. Test rolls count successes against a Goal, and are used for most uncertain tasks. Attack rolls compare a single total against a target’s Evasion, and are used for attacks.

Test Rolls

  1. The player describes their intent.
  2. The GM finalizes which Attribute and Skill fit.
  3. The GM sets a Goal for this test.
  4. The player rolls Attribute dice of the Skill’s size and counts successes against the Goal.

Goal

Goals are a number of successes required from a roll to have a chosen result occur. A goal’s difficulty is set in consideration of both the difficulty of the task itself and all of its surrounding context.

GoalDifficultyCharacter Reference
1TrivialThese tasks are stable and novice-level, only needing to be done when failure carries a relevant consequence. An untrained character fails this a little over half of the time.
2IntenseThese tasks are the working difficulty of a trained professional’s ordinary day. An average, competent character can be expected to succeed slightly more often than not, while the untrained likely can’t succeed at all.
3DemandingThese tasks are difficult tests for trained professionals. Competent characters fail these more often than they succeed, while specialists comfortably clear them.
4SevereThese tasks are complex and highly obstructed, and competence alone is not sufficient. Dedicated specialists can be expected to fail two times in three.
5ExtremeAt the practical ceiling of mortal capacity, only those dedicated purely to accomplishing these tasks can be expected to succeed a third of the time.
6+BeyondThese tasks are definitively the outermost edge of what the world actually allows.

Surpassing a Goal

Successes beyond a Goal generally matter for a variety of small aspects, much like an attack deals damage by surpassing Evasion with its Skill die + Attribute before adding its weapon die and other effects. Beyond that, extra successes are up to the GM, who is suggested to grant mild benefits and less consequence to the action.

Failing by more has no extra effect, and there is no partial success.

Opposed Rolls

A goal is set whenever a character acts within the context of an environment. When a character acts directly against another, this is an opposed roll, where both parties make their relevant rolls, and the higher success count wins. Examples include grapples, interrogations, and even debates, among much more.

Winning by extra successes has no further effect, unless a feature says otherwise.

Ties are broken via a game of random chance.

For quick reference (although this is explicitly only a suggestion, and alternatives are welcomed), if it is between the GM’s forces and a player’s, the player calls a face of a coin, and the GM flips it. If the face the player called is the one facing upward, the player’s forces win. If it is not, the GM’s forces win the tie instead. If it is between two players and their respective forces, the tie is broken by a best-of-three game of rock-paper-scissors.

When Not to Roll

When a character possesses overwhelming capability relevant to the situation, you can simply describe success and carry on. This also applies to contexts where failure wouldn’t meaningfully change anything. A roll is only warranted when consequences are meaningfully on the table, even if the players aren’t immediately aware of what the stakes at hand are.

Successes & Failure

  • 1-3 = Failure.
  • 4 = The success threshold. Rolling this or anything higher on a die qualifies it as a success.
  • Max Roll = Alongside the effects of rolling this face, roll one more die of the same size.
    • This is called an explosion. The explosion range of a die is “12 to 12” by default, and very difficult to increase. In this case, it means that you must roll a 12 or the die’s maximum face for your die to explode. If your explosion range would increase by 1, it’d become “11 to 12”, meaning any die would explode on an 11, 12, or the die’s maximum face, if that were not already 11 or 12.
    • If a die would explode but doesn’t clear the success threshold, then it does not explode.
    • A “full” explosion range means that as long as the die succeeds, it explodes.

Success Thresholds

Some effects raise or lower the Success Threshold. This changes only what counts as a success, which is 4 by default. It never changes what counts as a maximum face, so explosions are unaffected. The Threshold cannot be lowered below 3 or raised above 6 by any combination of effects.

Roll Augmentation

When a die is added to a roll, it is simply an extra die rolled alongside the others. Unless otherwise stated, it is treated as such, succeeding and exploding as expected.

When successes are dropped or raised, the count of successes is what’s affected, to a minimum of 0 and with no maximum.

A flat success is added to your success count without rolling a die, and cannot explode.

The faces of dice are not meant to be treated as values beyond whether or not they qualify to grant a success and/or explosion. An 11 on a d12 is not 11 successes under any circumstance, for example.

It is entirely common for values to be divided, which can result in fractional outcomes. Always round down, unless otherwise clarified.

Example

A character with Might 5 and Break d8 must make a Might-Break roll against a Goal 4.

  • As given by their stats, the character rolls 5d8: they roll an 8, 6, 4, 3, and 2. This is three total successes.
  • The 8 was a maximum face, and it explodes to roll another d8. It rolls an 8, which is another maximum face, granting another explosion.
  • The next d8 rolls a 2, which is a failure. This ends the chain, granting a total of 4 successes. The goal is met, and the character succeeds in accomplishing their goal.

Attack Rolls

Attack rolls are resolved differently from test rolls. Rather than counting successes against a Goal, they compare a single total against the target’s Evasion.

  1. Roll your weapon die and the Skill die called upon by your weapon.
  2. Add the Skill die’s result to your relevant Attribute. This is your attack total.
  3. If your attack total equals or exceeds the target’s Evasion, the attack hits.
  4. On a hit, your damage is the amount your attack total exceeded Evasion by, plus your weapon die’s result. Deal that damage and apply your effects.

Damage is then modified by the target’s Defense before it is allocated to their Periphery or Vitality.

Both the Skill die and the weapon die can explode, following the same explosion rules as Test rolls. When one explodes, roll another die of the same size and add it to that die’s result.

Example

A character with Might 4, Break d8, and a d8 weapon attacks a target with Evasion 6.

  • They roll a 5 on the Break die and add their Might of 4, for an attack total of 9. This exceeds the target’s Evasion by 3, so the attack hits.
  • They roll a 6 on the weapon die and add it to the 3, dealing 9 damage.
  • Had their attack total been exactly 6, the attack would still hit, dealing only the weapon die’s 6.

Provisioning

Goods are, by default, not assumed to be on hand. Acquiring them is its own roll, made during Downtimes or once per rest per good while somewhere that could possibly have a supply of them.

Every good has a price in Leptom and a Scarcity. Scarcity is whether you can find a good, and price is whether you can afford it. Scarcity is a Goal drawn from the standard difficulty table. A Scarcity 2 good is routine to come by when you know where you’re headed, and a Scarcity 6 good is functionally impossible to get your hands on.

It is important to select an approach. The following are examples of how to attempt to reach a good’s Scarcity:

  • Haggling (Speak): Pay full price.
  • Making (Craft): Pay half price in materials.
  • Exploring (Find): Pay nothing.
  • Stealing (Dodge): Pay nothing, but failures are the harshest.

Meeting the Goal yields 1 serving, unit, or whatever is sufficient. Surpassing the Goal does not innately yield more, but it does mean that you’ve likely found access to a consistent supplier. Failure consumes the attempt without yielding the sought-for goods. Failure could result in the exhaustion of sources, others becoming suspicious, or known sources making the goods harder for you to obtain, depending on the means.

Currency

The currency is Leptom. 1 day’s pay is about 3 Leptom.

Daily living is not tracked. The GM only charges for notable purchases.

Other Stats

Sizes

Entities possess a volume and three dimensions. An entity’s three dimensions are defined in depth (front to back), width (side to side), and height (bottom to top). An entity’s default dimensions are 1x1x1. An entity’s volume is their three dimensions multiplied together, by default being 1 as well.

While nuances are further decidable by the Game Master, it is recommended that the minimum threshold an entity can travel through (ie doorways or tunnel entrances) without being impeded is the product of the two lowest dimensions of their size.

Entities may travel through thresholds as small as a third of the product of their two lowest dimensions, being fallen over for the duration of fitting themself through it.

Carrying & Lifting

How much an entity can carry is at the GM’s discretion. After a certain point, it simply can no longer carry that much at once.

Lifting, shoving, or carrying something heavy, including another person, is a Might-Handle roll against a Goal set by its weight.

Move

Your Move is a series of values (each with an assigned series of tags) that determines the means by which you travel. (Speed) Walk is the default Move of an entity.

Tags

  • Walk - Movement along the top of the ground beneath you.
    • You may swim through water or similar fluids as if you had (Walk/2) Swim.
  • Swim - Movement through fluids, thin or thick.
  • Flight - Movement throughout gaseous materials, such as air.
    • At the end of each round, you must have moved at least an amount of spaces equal to your volume through flight that round, or you will begin to fall.
  • Hover - Movement through any gaseous medium, or any lack thereof.
  • Climb - Movement along the sides of surfaces.

Falling

When you fall, you take 1 damage to your Vitality for every space fallen beyond your Speed.

Defense

Your Defense is a list of conditional modifiers applied to each instance of incoming damage, before it is allocated to Vitality and Periphery. By default, your Defense is an empty box.

Typically, Defense is a negative number, implying a subtractive modifier to the total numbers, but many options are available for Defense properties. If damage is ever reduced, it is rounded up to the nearest whole number. For example, if you have the following defense:
-(Might) Blunt, +1 Knives, /2 Puncture

  • If you took 9 blunt and had 3 Might, it’d become 6.
  • If you took 9 slice from a knife slashing at you, it’d become 10 slice.
  • If you took 9 puncture, it’d become 5.

Multiple Defense modifiers stack in an order optimal for the defender to transform it to the least damage. Damage is resolved before it is allocated to Periphery or Vitality, but after the total output is calculated. There is no minimum damage.

Damage Types

Blunt, Slice, Puncture, Chemical, Cold, Heat, Electric, Sonic, Holy, Necrotic, Psychic, and Anomalous.

Evasion

An entity’s Evasion is equal to 6 at a baseline, and is increased by means of features, equipment, and environmental factors.

Periphery

Periphery is the surface-level avoidance and ability to keep damage from truly making harmful contact. It represents everything about the avoidance of real hits, including skill and luck.

By default, your Periphery (and your maximum Periphery) is equal to 2*(your Speed + your Mind).

  • When you take damage, it is dealt to your Periphery by default. Any amount may be allocated to Vitality instead, at your volition. Damage is dealt to your Vitality if your Periphery is not a potential target.
  • If Periphery reaches 0, it becomes broken (or breaks, both terms are used). While your Periphery is broken, damage may not be allocated to it, it may not be reduced below 0, and effects will all treat it as 0. Nothing may modify your Periphery’s value while it is broken, unless it explicitly lifts the condition of it being broken.
  • When damage would break your Periphery while you aren’t surprised, you may enact a Periphery Cleanup, which lowers the amount of damage that would’ve been dealt to your Vitality by your maximum Periphery.
  • Periphery may be raised above its maximum, up to double its maximum, but only by effects that explicitly grant excess. Means of natural bodily recovery always stops at your maximum.

Vitality

Your Vitality is the meat, your capacity for actual harm.

By default, your Vitality (and your maximum Vitality) is equal to 2*(your Might + your Grit).

When you are reduced to 0 Vitality, you are killed.

Momentum

Momentum is a resource spent on effects outside of the typical turn economy, such as reacting, intercepting, and making smaller actions without losing your turn.

Your maximum Momentum is equal to your Charm.

  • When combat begins, you start with your maximum Momentum. If you are surprised, you start with 0 instead.
  • Momentum does not refresh. It is only regained by Honing, which restores 2 Momentum, up to your maximum.

Death

In Epitaph, a character dies when they reach 0 Vitality.

Combat

Combat is meant to be extraordinarily fast, with the GM actively calling out each Count, and players raising their hand when they have a card for it.

Scenes & Encounters

A scene is a grander description, while an encounter is a smaller moment, like a fight, in that same environment the scene is taking place across.

Starting & Ending Combat

The GM decides when combat begins and deals the first round. As a general rule of thumb, combat begins upon the initial hostile actions being taken. The first hostile action occurs before combat begins.

A participant unaware of hostility is automatically surprised. Surprised participants still take turns, but begin with no Momentum.

An entity joining combat partway through is dealt in at the start of the next round, since each round is approximately a single second.

You leave combat once it completely concludes, or once you are so out of the way that nobody could realistically reach you. Beginning to run and making good progress doesn’t mean you’ve left, since someone still fighting could simply teleport to follow you.

Combat typically ends once no remaining entities are hostile.

Dealing In

Each round, every participant is dealt a number of cards from a standard deck. Each card is one turn.

ParticipantCards
Player characters2
Fodder & non-participants: groups of NPCs highly incompetent in combat, such as minions or civilians1 per group, acting all at once
Lesser threats: NPCs capable of being an individual threat, but noticeably lesser than the PCs1 each
Fair matches: NPCs who are a fair match for a given PC’s competency2 each
Greater threats: NPCs noticeably greater than the PCs in power3 each

Features may temporarily change how many cards a participant is dealt. Typically, no more than 26 cards are dealt in a round.

Jokers do nothing by default. Optionally, Jokers may be included in the deck, acting as Aces and granting 1 Momentum to whoever draws them.

Rounds & Counts

A round is a single, full loop of combat, made up of individual steps called Counts. Counts descend from 10 to 1 and repeat each round. You take a turn on the Count matching each card you hold, so a player character takes two turns per round by default.

CardCount
Ace, King, Queen, Jack, 1010, acting in that order
2–9Equal to the card’s value

A participant holding an Ace may forgo their turn at Count 10 to act at Count 1 instead.

Ties of the same card are broken by suit, in reverse alphabetical order: spades first, hearts second, diamonds third, and clubs last.

Running a Round

  1. The GM calls out each Count, starting from 10.
  2. Players with a card for that Count raise their hand.
  3. A player takes their turn, says that their turn has ended, and turns that card over.
  4. A player who has finished all of their turns takes the deck and all turned-in cards, and begins reshuffling while the round completes.
  5. At the end of the round, the cards are returned to the GM, who cuts them.

On A Turn

You may spend your turn taking any of the following actions, or an action granted to you by an external factor to this list. An effect marked Turn is such an action, and using it takes your turn.

  • Attack: Make an attack roll.
  • Grab: With a free hand, make an opposed roll against an entity within reach. If you win, they are grabbed by you.
  • Break Free: Make an opposed roll against your grabber, or a roll against the Goal of an object holding you. If you win, you are no longer grabbed.
  • Spellcast: Cast a spell you have access to that lists its time of casting to be a turn.
  • Hide: Attempt to use an obstruction, darkness, or sufficient distraction to conceal yourself from being in a known position to anyone who may be looking for you. Make a Dodge roll. Your successes become the Goal for any Find roll made to perceive you, including later Searches.
    • Entities who knew of your location and were actively paying attention to you cannot be fooled by this roll.
    • Entities who knew of your location before this but weren’t actively paying you direct attention must immediately make a Find roll, failing to immediately perceive you if they do not meet that Goal.
    • While hidden in this way:
      • Unperceiving entities cannot know where you currently are through perceptive means or target you until they reattempt (and succeed) the roll through a Search.
      • You may attack an entity from this position (or make yourself known through other means), ceasing to be hidden. They are considered surprised by your abilities without inflicting them with the condition.
  • Move: Travel in spaces equal to your Move, using your means of movement.
  • Sprint: Move up to five times your Move. You cannot Sprint again until you have Honed, and you may only Sprint a number of times per breather equal to your Grit.
  • Retrieve: Pick up or draw an apparent, unrestrained item within reach.
  • Search: Make a Find roll against anything hidden in your perceivable space. Speed or Mind are always accessible for Search actions, while other Attributes are subject to GM approval.
  • Hone: Regain 2 Momentum, and recharge features relying on it, such as sprinting.

Spending Momentum

Interruptions

By default, an Interruption costs Momentum in response to a set trigger. An effect marked Interruption (# Momentum) is something you do at that cost in response to the trigger that follows it. An Interruption (0 Momentum) costs no Momentum, and may be used up to once per turn. Common Interruptions include:

  • Block (1 Momentum): Double your Defense against an incoming instance of damage. Only modifiers that reduce damage are doubled, so -3 becomes -6 and /2 becomes /4. Modifiers that increase damage are unchanged.
  • Sidestep (1 Momentum): Increase your Evasion by 2 against an incoming attack.

Block and Sidestep are declared after an attack’s total is called out, but before its damage is rolled.

You may respond to the same trigger multiple times, but not with the same feature.

On Your Turn

If an effect simply reads “During your turn, you may spend X Momentum to ___,” it is effectively an action on your turn that costs Momentum instead of your turn. Many actions can be taken this way:

ActionMomentum
Move2
Search2
Retrieve1
Shift1

Shift is exclusively a Momentum expense on your turn. It moves you 1 space, and cannot be spent standing up.

Weapons

Weapons are written as Name | Attribute | Skill | Die | Type | Reach or Range | Properties. A weapon’s die is its only damage value, and its properties are what set it apart.

Each weapon lists one or two Attributes. Its attack rolls may only use one of those Attributes.

Reach

Distance is measured in spaces, with 1 space being 5 feet. Reach 1 covers every space adjacent to you, diagonals included. Each additional point of Reach extends one space further in every direction.

Range

Ranged weapons have a Range, the farthest number of spaces they can attack. When the target of a ranged attack is in melee with you, your attack total is decreased by the target’s Speed.

Ammunition is a provisioned good. It is generally tracked, but not down to individual values. Weapons that must be reloaded have the Ammo property.

A thrown weapon lands near its target. Getting it back is a Retrieve once it is within reach.

Shields

Shields are weapons, attacked with like any other. Their properties are their defensive aspects.

Unarmed

Attacks made without a weapon are Unarmed | Might/Speed | Break | 0 | Blunt | Reach 1.

Hands

You have 2 hands, and each holds one object. A weapon without Bimanual takes one hand.

Letting go of an object costs nothing. Drawing, stowing, or regripping with both hands is a Retrieve.

Unarmed attacks and spellcasting do not need a free hand.

Cover

An obstruction between an attacker and their target grants the target cover against that attack.

CoverObstructionEffect
Weak CoverConceals about half of the target, such as a low wall, a tree trunk, or another entity+2 Evasion
Strong CoverConceals most of the target, such as an arrow slit or a window frame+4 Evasion

A target fully concealed by an obstruction cannot be targeted by attacks through it.

Mounts & Vehicles

A trained mount can be commanded and controlled, but can technically act on its own volition, such as when control is taken away by fear. An untrained mount grants no control of any sort.

An attacker chooses whether to target the rider or the mount. The type of mount determines the cover it grants the rider.

When your mount falls over, make a Speed-Handle roll against a Goal of 2. When you are staggered while riding, make a Speed-Handle roll against a Goal equal to your stacks of Staggered. If you fail either, you land fallen over in an adjacent space.

Vehicles, such as carts and boats, are objects with a Move, steered by a Handle roll from whoever drives them on their turn. Passengers act normally and move with the vehicle.

Armor

Recovery & Downtime

Honing Outside of Combat

Outside of combat, a Hone takes a few seconds of stillness.

Breather

A breather is around 30 minutes of light, stationary activity or sleep. It restores your Periphery as listed in Recovering Periphery.

You may take any number of breathers, but a breather does not reset your rest timer.

Rest

A rest is at least 8 hours of heavy sleep. When you finish a rest:

  • Your Periphery is restored to its maximum, and is no longer broken.
  • You restore 1 Vitality.
  • Your rest timer resets.

Falling Asleep

A rest can be attempted once per hour. Its Goal starts at 0 and is modified by the space you are resting in. If the Goal is 1 or higher, make a Mind or Grit roll with Resist or Treat to fall asleep. Otherwise, no roll is needed.

FactorModifiers
BeddingLuxurious bed -2, ordinary bedding +0, improvised bedding (such as foliage) +1, no bedding +2
TemperaturePerfectly suited -1, ordinary +0, mildly unpleasant +1, biologically incompatible heat or cold +3
ThreatsExtraordinarily safe -1, typical structure +0, threatening cues (such as distant howling) +1, known active danger +2

Rest Timer

You must finish a rest within 14 + (Grit x 2) hours of finishing your previous one. If you don’t, you gain a stack of Fatigue and your rest timer restarts.

Treatment

During a rest, a caretaker may spend 30 minutes to make a Mind-Treat roll against a Goal of 2 for a patient. On a success, the patient restores 1 additional Vitality from that rest. A patient can only be treated once per rest, and only one treatment effect can take place on a patient at a time.

Without supplies usable for treatment, such as a medical kit or an herbalist kit, the roll is made with 1 fewer die. Supplies cost 6 Leptom, have Scarcity 2, and last for 5 treatments.

Out of combat, a successful treatment during a breather may instead remove 1 stack of Fatigue from the patient.

During the Recuperate downtime activity, a caretaker’s successful treatment restores 1 additional Vitality that day.

Treatment rules will eventually be integrated in combat and other circumstances.

Survival

Breath

You can hold your breath for Grit x 10 rounds. Your remaining rounds are counted down at the end of each round. When you take damage to your Vitality, your remaining rounds are reduced by 5 times the damage taken to your Vitality.

When you run out, you gain 1 stack of Fatigue, and then also take 1 damage to your Vitality at the end of each round until you can breathe.

Food & Water

Each day without water, you gain 1 stack of Fatigue.

Without food, you gain 1 stack of Fatigue for each day after Grit days.

Temperature

Each hour in biologically incompatible heat or cold, make a Grit-Resist roll against a Goal of 2, or higher depending on severity. If you fail, you gain 1 stack of Fatigue. Gear or shelter suited to the climate lets you skip the roll.

Diseases will be designed as conditions once lore is confirmed.

Downtime

Downtime is an extended period, ranging anywhere from days to years. Each day of downtime, a character chooses one activity. Within the narrative, this is unlikely to be all they do, but only that activity has a mechanical effect.

TrainYou may spend progression points to increase Might, Speed, or Grit.

Choose a Skill and make a roll with it using Mind or Grit, recording your successes. Once a Skill’s recorded successes reach the size of its next die (6 for d6, up to 12 for d12), you may spend progression points to purchase it. Recorded successes persist between downtimes and are tracked per Skill.

Extended projects, relationships, and reputation are ruled by the GM rather than by set mechanics. There are no particular limits on how many characters can work toward the same project.

Each tool will have full crafting mechanics unique per-tool.

Character Creation

The following is the process for creating a level 1 character.

1. Class

Take 1 level in a class, apply what it grants, and choose one of its level 1 variant features.

2. Species & Ancestry

Choose a species and one of its ancestries, if it has any, and apply their traits and bonuses.

3. Origin

Choose an origin, and apply its traits and bonuses.

4. Attributes

All of your Attributes begin at 2. Choose 3 different Attributes and raise each by 1. Then, choose 2 Attributes and raise each by 1. Finally, apply any Attribute bonuses granted by your other choices.

An Attribute’s maximum is 5 by default, and can be increased with progression points.

5. Skills

All Skills begin at d4, and are then affected by any Skill bonuses granted by your other choices. You then gain 14 points to spend on Skills, with each die step costing 2. Points left unspent are lost.

Advancement

Characters progress from level 1 to level 12 whenever they achieve massive milestones, as dictated by the GM in conference with their players.

Gaining a Level

Each level gained is placed into a class. The GM may veto leveling into a class that does not make narrative sense for the character.

For each level gained, you gain that class’s core feature for your new level in it.

Each class level also makes two of that class’s variant features available to you. For each level gained, choose one variant feature from all of those available to you that you have not yet picked. Variant features you pass on remain available at later levels.

As a suggestion for GMs, and not a rule that you must follow, a character may have acquired 120 Leptom x their level each level up. This is cumulative, and can be spent on things to start off with.

Progression Points

Each time you gain a level, you gain progression points equal to your new level. They may be banked freely, and are spent during downtime.

Multiclassing

  • Core features are tied to your level in that class, rather than your total level.
  • Features referring to “your (class name) level” refer to your level in that class. Features referring to “your level” refer to your total level.
  • Resources from different classes are tracked separately and do not pool together in any way.

Magic

By default, you cannot cast spells. Spells are only gained through features, and the feature granting a spell states what casting it costs.

Each spell states how it resolves, whether through an attack roll, an opposed roll, or by simply taking effect.

Casting Time

Every spell lists its time of casting.

TimeCasting
TurnCast with the Spellcast action.
InterruptionCast in response to the trigger the spell lists, spending the Momentum it lists.
ExtendedCast across the Counts or minutes the spell lists, maintaining Focus throughout. The spell takes effect once that time has passed.

Extended casting uses only the turn you began casting it on.

Enacting, Triggering, and Focus

Timed effects are triggered (have their effect take place) asynchronously from when they’re enacted (caused or used by the wielder of the timed effect).

For example, an ability enacted on Count 5 that triggers in 3 Counts triggers on Count 2. An ability enacted on Count 2 that triggers in 3 Counts triggers on Count 9 next round.

Timed effects trigger at the end of the Count they occur on, after all other non-timed effects and turns on that Count have resolved thoroughly.

Features that have their effects occur based on set conditions are not timed effects, but instead conditional effects. These have no countdown, and are simply expected to occur at the very moment the effect that triggered them resolves.

Timed effects (and many spells) may make use of Focus, a mechanic explicitly tied to maintaining whatever permits the effect to develop over its dormant duration into successfully taking place. As some examples:

  • A Paladin uses Guardian Touch, and Focuses on continuing to touch the entity they targeted for 3 Counts to trigger it. If they lose this Focus, they don’t regain any specific resource pools they already expended to enact the effect, nor do they regain the time spent attempting to maintain the Focus.
    • If the Paladin is forcefully pulled away from the target, the Focus is broken and the timed effect does not succeed in taking place.
    • If the Paladin is forcefully pulled away from the target, returns in less than 3 Counts, and touches the entity again, the Focus does not return, and the timed effect still fails to take place.
    • If the Paladin takes damage to their Periphery but maintains the touch for all 3 Counts, the Focus isn’t broken, since no prerequisite failed to be met. As such, the timed effect succeeds and takes place.

Focus

Some spells require Focus to maintain their effects or to finish being cast. While you maintain Focus, the spell’s targets or targeted space must remain able to be targeted as the spell requires.

Focus breaks, ending the spell, when:

  • You become unconscious.
  • You take damage to your Vitality.
  • You choose to end it.
  • The spell’s targets or targeted space can no longer be targeted.
  • An effect breaks it.

You may maintain Focus on one spell at a time.

Leagues

Spells are graded in Leagues from 1st to 4th, dictating their potency.

You may upcast a spell, casting it at a higher League than its own, as long as you could cast a spell of that League. Its bracketed values {2nd, 3rd, 4th} replace its base values according to the League it is cast at. An upcast spell is treated as its new League for its costs and all other effects.

Upcast values ramp up sharply, borderline exponentially, with each League, such as 1 {3, 8, 20}.

Anima

Anima are the conditions under which a spell can be drawn upon. A spell may list an Anima, and it cannot be cast unless that condition is true. Anima is only checked at the moment of casting. If the condition stops being true afterward, the spell is not undone.

AnimaCondition
FervorYou have taken damage to your Vitality this combat.
HealthYour Vitality is at its maximum, or above the spell’s League squared (1, 4, 9, or 16).
VengeanceThe targets, or their allies, have harmed you or one of your allies this scene.
WillYou are not currently maintaining Focus on anything.
ZealotryYou are not frightened or hypnotized.

Bleeding

Bleeding

Bleeding stacks.

  • At the end of each round, you lose 1 Vitality.
  • While your Vitality is below your stacks, you are unconscious.
  • When you would gain Vitality, your stacks of Bleeding are removed instead.

Blinded

  • Your attack rolls are decreased by 4 whenever hitting the target would use sight at all. Avoiding this through hearing requires a feature.

Burning

  • At the end of each round, you take 2 Heat to your Vitality.
  • Spending a turn, or 2 Momentum, ends this condition.

Deafened

Deafened

  • You cannot perceive by sound.
  • You automatically fail all rolls requiring hearing, such as hearing someone speak to you, even against a Goal of 0.

Fallen Over

Fallen Over

  • Your Evasion is halved against melee attacks, and increased by 2 against ranged attacks.
  • Your melee reach is halved in spaces, rounded up.
  • Your Move is halved.
  • You can stand when you move, spending all of your Move to do so. This ends the condition.
  • Only entities on a surface can be fallen over.

Fatigue

Fatigue

Fatigue stacks.

  • All incoming damage is increased by 1 per stack.
  • Your Move is decreased by 1 space per stack, to a minimum of 1. While your Move is 1 or lower, you cannot Sprint.
  • Your Test rolls lose 1 success per stack.
  • You are never considered to beat a Goal by more than 5 minus your stacks.
  • If your stacks equal or exceed your Grit + 2, you die.
  • Whenever you gain Vitality, you lose 1 stack, regardless of how much Vitality you gain.
  • If you would gain Vitality but cannot due to being at your maximum, your stacks are halved, rounded down.
Link to original

Frightened

Frightened

Frightened stacks, and its stacks are tracked separately for each source of fear.

  • Test rolls targeting or resisting the source are made with 1 less die per stack, to a minimum of 1 die. Attack rolls against the source take -1 to the attack total per stack.
  • You cannot willingly move closer to the source.
  • You cannot Hone.
  • If your stacks exceed your Mind, you must spend each of your turns moving away from where you believe the source to be, and you may only spend Momentum to move away from it.
  • At the end of each round in which you cannot perceive the source, you lose 1 stack. If the source is destroyed, all stacks end.
Link to original

Grabbed

Grabbed

  • You cannot move away from your grabber. When your grabber moves, they may drag you with them at half their Move.
  • Your Evasion is halved.
  • You may Break Free on your turn. An object holding you, such as cuffs or glue, has a Goal to beat instead of an opposed roll.
  • The grab ends if your grabber lets go, is moved away from you, or cannot act.
Link to original

Hypnosis

Hypnosis

When you are hypnotized without clarification as to which aspect of hypnosis you are affected by, the context surrounding the effect is the aspect. Otherwise, you are inflicted with the respective aspects of hypnosis.

  • Charmed: You cannot willingly target the source with any attacks or harmful effects. You cannot negatively impact the source in any way as you understand it, including harming their allies.
  • Compelled: You change sides, becoming an ally to the source. Your turn is dictated by the source. If it does not have an ability to dictate this for you, you instead behave normally, as if the source is still your ally (for example, maybe only trying to talk your former allies down rather than striking them). You are, however, violently defensive on behalf of the source, keeping the source protected by whatever means are sensible to you.
  • Transfixed: You cannot willingly look away or turn your attention away from the source. You may not spend Momentum.

Invisible

Invisible

  • You cannot be perceived by sight.
  • You are hidden, as with the Hide action.
  • You gain 2 flat successes on rolls made to avoid being perceived.

Staggered

Staggered

Staggered stacks.

  • Your Evasion is reduced by 1 per stack, to a minimum of 0.
  • Test rolls made to resist effects lose 1 success per stack.
  • If you reach 6 stacks, you become fallen over.
  • At the end of each round, you lose 1 stack.

Surprised

Surprised

When you are surprised, you were unprepared for an event as it transpires before you.

  • You cannot spend Momentum.
  • You cannot enact a Periphery Cleanup.
  • Surprised ends once the first round of combat has passed.

An entity considered surprised by an attack suffers these effects against that attack only, without gaining the condition.

Link to original

Unconscious

Unconscious

  • When you are unconscious, you become fallen over.
  • You cannot take turns, act, or think, and you cannot gain or spend Momentum.
  • Natural unconsciousness (such as sleep) can be ended by loud noises, being pushed, or taking damage. More imposing means of unconsciousness, such as conditions or spells, require their own unique ends to be met to conclude the effect.
